My Wife Set Up a Claude Code Subscription Today

August 3, 2026

Small milestone in our house today: my wife signed up for a Claude Code subscription. Nothing dramatic happened — no confetti, no big announcement — but it felt worth writing down, because it's the kind of quiet decision that tends to change how the next few months actually go.

She'd been circling the idea for a while. Watching me poke at side projects, hearing me complain about boilerplate, and asking the occasional "wait, it can do that?" when I showed her something. Eventually the curiosity won out over the wait-and-see, and this morning she just went ahead and got the subscription.

Why now

There wasn't one single trigger. It was more a pile-up of small reasons: a work project that kept stalling on tedious setup steps, a few ideas she'd been sitting on that never made it past "I should build that someday," and honestly, some FOMO from watching how much faster my own workflow had gotten. At some point the cost of not trying it started to outweigh the cost of just signing up.

She's not a professional developer — more of a "gets things done, picks up tools as needed" person — so the appeal wasn't really about writing production-grade software. It was about having something that could take a vague idea and turn it into working code without needing to relearn syntax she'd half-forgotten from years ago.

The first hour

We spent the first hour just getting the basics running: installing the CLI, connecting it to a project folder, and figuring out the difference between asking it to explain something versus asking it to actually change files. She was cautious at first, the way anyone is with a new tool that has real access to their computer, but the permission prompts helped — she liked being asked before anything happened rather than discovering changes after the fact.

The moment that seemed to land for her was watching it read through an existing folder of scripts, explain what they did, and then fix a bug she'd been avoiding for weeks. Not because the fix was complicated, but because it removed the "ugh, I have to sit down and figure this out" barrier that had been the actual obstacle the whole time.

What's next

No grand plans yet. Just a list of small annoyances she's been meaning to fix and a couple of half-formed project ideas she wants to poke at without the usual setup dread. I'm mostly looking forward to comparing notes — two people in the same house, using the same tool, probably running into completely different problems.

Will report back once she's actually shipped something.
